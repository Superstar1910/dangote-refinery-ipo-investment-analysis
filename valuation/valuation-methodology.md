# Valuation Methodology and Limitations

## Methods

The research triangulates value using:

- Discounted cash flow for the operating asset;
- EV/EBITDA and P/E comparisons with listed refiners;
- Replacement-cost and capacity cross-checks;
- A risk-adjusted sum-of-the-parts approach separating proven operations from the proposed expansion;
- Four probability-weighted scenarios; and
- Reverse valuation to identify assumptions embedded in the ₦525 offer price.

## Central parameters

The operating-asset framework used a 15–17% USD WACC, with higher discount rates for uncommitted expansion cash flows. The published sensitivity indicated that a WACC near 12% could mathematically support approximately ₦525, but the analysis did not consider that rate supportable given sovereign, FX, leverage, governance, liquidity and execution risks.

The preliminary fair-value range of ₦287–₦372 is not a point estimate. The probability-weighted present value was approximately ₦333. The disciplined entry threshold around ₦280 was derived by requiring a margin of safety and an expected three-year return around the portfolio hurdle, rather than simply buying at estimated fair value.

## Key limitations

- H1 2026 performance may not represent a through-cycle refining environment.
- The refinery had a limited audited operating history.
- Expansion financing, timing, dilution and cost-overrun allocation were not fully resolved.
- No reliable post-listing price, turnover or liquidity evidence existed at the research cut-off.
- Naira hedging costs were not based on executable dealer quotes; a 300–500bp drag is better viewed as a partial-hedge or portfolio assumption, not the cost of a fully hedged position.
- Peer businesses differ in geography, complexity, tax, leverage, product slate and governance.
- Prospectus inconsistencies reduce confidence in selected disclosures.

All valuations are analyst estimates, sensitive to utilisation, crack spreads, working capital, capex, FX, financing terms, WACC and terminal assumptions.
